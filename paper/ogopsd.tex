\begin{abstract}
On-policy self-distillation (OPSD) trains one language model as both student and teacher. The student samples a solution from the problem alone; the teacher, which also sees the reference solution, supplies token-level supervision on that same trajectory. The original loss never checks whether the sampled answer is correct. We multiply that loss by a sequence weight from a boxed-answer verifier: a completed wrong answer is upweighted, while truncated traces and already-correct traces stay at weight one. On Qwen3-1.7B with a 100-step LoRA budget, the change lifts HMMT25 Avg@12 from 22.8 to 27.8 relative to a reproduced OPSD run. AIME24 and AIME25 stay at or below that run. An ablation that also upweights truncations is reported alongside the default boxed-error rule.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Post-training for mathematical reasoning is usually some mix of supervised imitation, reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR), and distillation from a stronger teacher \cite{hinton2015distilling,ouyang2022instructgpt,shao2024deepseekmath,agarwal2024gkd}. Supervised fine-tuning is off-policy. Group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) and related RLVR methods give a sequence-level scalar, and the gradient vanishes when a group is uniformly correct or uniformly wrong \cite{shao2024deepseekmath,deepseekai2025r1}. Distillation is denser, but the usual recipes need a separate teacher and often train on teacher-generated text.

OPSD \cite{zhao2026opsd} drops the external teacher. One network plays both roles through different prompts: the student sees the problem, and the teacher also sees a reference solution while scoring the student's own tokens. Training uses one rollout per problem and a few hundred optimizer steps. The authors report large gains on AIME and HMMT with Qwen3 models.

Two observations motivate the present work. First, OPSD's loss does not look at whether the student answer is right. The original paper lists this as a limitation: the framework ``does not explicitly leverage correctness verification of generated answers.'' Second, a single-GPU reproduction of the Qwen3-1.7B recipe recovers the paper's AIME24/25 step-100 numbers to about one point, but misses the reported HMMT25 gain ($23.1 \rightarrow 29.2$). Local OPSD is essentially flat on HMMT ($23.06 \rightarrow 22.78$). If a correctness signal is going to matter, this is the suite where it should show up.

After each student rollout we compute a binary reward $R$ with the same boxed-answer extractor and \texttt{math-verify} checker used at evaluation, then scale the original per-token KL by a sequence weight $w(R)$. The teacher, LoRA layout, clip, and sampling protocol stay as in OPSD. Upweighting every incorrect rollout also upweights truncated completions that never boxed an answer; with a 1024-token cap and student thinking turned off, those traces are common. The default rule upweights only \emph{completed but incorrect} rollouts. We call this \textbf{boxed-error reweighting} (BER-OPSD). Concurrent work by Wang et al.\ uses the name Outcome-Guided On-Policy Self-Distillation and the abbreviation OG-OPSD for a different method that switches the divergence and the distilled prefix using outcome rewards and teacher entropy \cite{wang2026ogopsd}. The KL term here is unchanged; only the sequence weight depends on $R$.

On the same 1$\times$A800, 100-step LoRA budget used for the OPSD reproduction, boxed-error reweighting raises HMMT25 Avg@12 by $5.0$ points over local OPSD and $4.7$ points over the base model. AIME24 and AIME25 fall slightly. The rest of the paper reports all three suites and both weighting rules.

\section{Background}

\paragraph{On-policy distillation.}
Given a teacher $p_T$ and student $p_S$, sequence-level distillation matches next-token distributions \cite{kim2016sequencekd,gu2024minillm}. On-policy variants sample $y \sim p_S(\cdot\mid x)$ and apply the divergence on those tokens, reducing exposure bias \cite{agarwal2024gkd}. OPSD instantiates both distributions from one model $p_\theta$ by changing only the prompt: $p_S(\cdot\mid x)=p_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ and $p_T(\cdot\mid x,y^\star)=p_\theta(\cdot\mid x,y^\star)$, with $y^\star$ a reference solution. The student generates $\hat y\sim p_S$; both heads then evaluate $\hat y$, and training minimizes a token-averaged forward KL along $\hat y$ (the released code uses $\beta{=}0$ in a generalized JSD, i.e.\ $\mathrm{KL}(p_T\|p_S)$), with a per-token clip that stops stylistic tokens from dominating \cite{zhao2026opsd}.

\paragraph{Verifiable rewards.}
RLVR assigns $R\in\{0,1\}$ by checking a boxed answer against gold \cite{cobbe2021gsm8k,shao2024deepseekmath}. GRPO estimates advantages within a group of rollouts; the gradient vanishes when all rewards in the group are equal. OPSD is dense and uses one sample, with no outcome check. BER-OPSD keeps that single rollout and the same KL, and uses $R$ only as a sequence weight.

\section{Boxed-Error Reweighting}

Let $D_{\mathrm{KL}}^{(n)}$ be the clipped token KL at position $n$ of a student completion $\hat y$. Standard OPSD uses
\begin{equation}
\ell(\hat y)=\frac{1}{|\hat y|}\sum_{n} D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\big(p_T(\cdot\mid x,y^\star,\hat y_{<n})\;\|\;p_S(\cdot\mid x,\hat y_{<n})\big).
\end{equation}
We leave this term unchanged and multiply it by a sequence weight. After decoding $\hat y$ we extract the last $\backslash\texttt{boxed}\{\cdot\}$ span (searching after \texttt{</think>} when present) and set $R=\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{verify}(\hat y,y^\star)]$, using \texttt{math-verify} with a normalized-string fallback. Let $B=\mathbf{1}[\hat y\text{ contains a boxed span}]$. Two gates are compared:
\begin{align}
w_{\mathrm{all}} &= 1+\lambda(1-R), \label{eq:all}\\
w_{\mathrm{box}} &= 1+\lambda\, B\,(1-R). \label{eq:box}
\end{align}
The training loss is $\mathbb{E}[w\,\ell(\hat y)]$. When $w\equiv 1$ the estimator is identical to OPSD, including the token-micro average: sequence weights broadcast over tokens, so all-ones recovers the original reduction.

Equation~\eqref{eq:all} (\textsc{All-Error}) doubles every incorrect sequence, including truncated text with $B{=}0$. In our 100-step runs the boxed rate is about $0.48$ and the true-correct rate about $0.37$, so roughly half of the extra mass lands on incomplete prefixes. Equation~\eqref{eq:box} (\textsc{Boxed-Error}, the default) applies the extra factor only to completed-but-wrong attempts. Truncations keep weight $1$; already-correct rollouts also keep weight $1$, so the teacher can still reshape style on successes. We use $\lambda{=}1$ unless noted. Algorithm~\ref{alg:og} is the training step; lines after the OPSD rollout are the only addition.

\begin{algorithm}[tb]
\caption{BER-OPSD training step}
\label{alg:og}
\textbf{Input}: batch $\{(x_i,y_i^\star)\}$, $p_\theta$, $\lambda$\\
\textbf{Output}: reweighted KL loss
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\FORALL{$(x,y^\star)$ in the minibatch}
\STATE Sample $\hat y\sim p_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ \COMMENT{student; no gold}
\STATE Teacher logits: $p_\theta(\cdot\mid x,y^\star)$ with LoRA disabled
\STATE Student logits: $p_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ with LoRA enabled
\STATE $\ell \leftarrow$ clipped token $\mathrm{KL}(p_T\|p_S)$ on $\hat y$
\STATE $R\leftarrow\mathrm{verify}(\hat y,y^\star)$;\; $B\leftarrow\mathbf{1}[\text{boxed}]$
\STATE $w\leftarrow 1+\lambda(1-R)$ or $1+\lambda B(1-R)$
\STATE accumulate $w\cdot\ell$
\ENDFOR
\STATE update LoRA parameters of $p_\theta$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

There is no group, no advantage normalization, and no policy-gradient term on $R$. Gradients still flow through the student logits of the KL. $R$ is treated as a constant with respect to $\theta$, since the discrete verifier is non-differentiable. The implementation adds one flag to the public OPSD trainer; $\lambda{=}0$ recovers the original path.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Protocol}

All methods use Qwen3-1.7B \cite{yang2025qwen3} with LoRA rank $64$, $\alpha{=}128$, dropout $0.05$, on the OpenThoughts math split of $29{,}434$ problems \cite{openthoughts2025}. We follow the OPSD 1.7B recipe: student thinking off, teacher thinking on, fixed teacher (base weights, adapters disabled), forward KL, token clip $0.05$, temperature $1.1$ at train time, max completion $1024$, max length $20{,}000$, learning rate $5{\times}10^{-6}$, per-device batch $2$, gradient accumulation $2$ (effective batch $4$), $100$ steps, vLLM colocated rollouts \cite{kwon2023vllm,hu2022lora}. Training runs on one A800 80GB GPU in about $20$ minutes. The original OPSD numbers were trained on $4{\times}$H100 with effective batch $32$. The comparisons below are against a reproduction trained and evaluated under the same 1-GPU budget.

Evaluation matches the OPSD README: thinking on, temperature $1.0$, $\mathrm{top}\text{-}p{=}0.95$, $\mathrm{top}\text{-}k$ off, $38{,}912$ new tokens, $12$ samples per problem, Avg@12 as the primary metric, plus Pass@12 and majority-vote@12. Suites are AIME24, AIME25, and HMMT February 2025, $30$ problems each \cite{aime2024,hmmt2025}. Checkpoints are merged into the base before serving. Following the OPSD README we use a single decoding seed; Avg@12 typically moves by a point or two across runs.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{Base} is the unmodified Qwen3-1.7B instruct model. \emph{OPSD} is our 100-step reproduction (\texttt{repro\_100\_gpu0}). A 100-step GRPO run with 1024-token completions would not match the official GRPO recipe (eight $16$k-token rollouts per prompt), so we leave GRPO out. SFT on the same traces shortens test-time reasoning in the OPSD paper; we did not rerun it.

\textbf{Boxed-Error} is the default method. Relative to local OPSD it moves HMMT25 from $22.8$ to $27.8$ Avg@12 ($82/360$ correct solutions to $100/360$), Pass@12 from $43.3$ to $56.7$, and majority vote from $26.7$ to $33.3$ (Table~\ref{tab:aux}). The paper's 4-GPU OPSD score on this suite is $29.2$. AIME24 drops $0.8$ and AIME25 drops $3.9$ Avg@12. The three-suite mean is $40.5$ versus OPSD's $40.4$; over $1080$ graded solutions the counts are $437$ versus $436$.

\textbf{All-Error} upweights truncated text as well. It is the only variant that beats local OPSD on AIME24 ($57.5$ vs.\ $56.1$, Pass@12 $80.0$ vs.\ $76.7$). It loses $2.2$ on AIME25, gains $0.8$ on HMMT, and ties OPSD on the macro mean and on the $1080$-solution count ($436$--$436$). Extra loss mass therefore lands on prefixes that never boxed an answer, so we keep Boxed-Error as the default.

OPSD and both reweighted runs stay well above the base model on AIME (roughly $+5$ to $+8$ Avg@12).

\subsection{Training Signal}

On-policy completions in this recipe are short and often unfinished. Across the All-Error run the mean boxed rate is $0.48$ and the mean verified-correct rate is $0.37$. The Boxed-Error run logs a boxed-error rate (completed-but-wrong) around $0.10$--$0.13$ per logging window, so the extra weight is sparse. Firing on about one in eight traces concentrates the extra loss on completed mistakes rather than on the large truncated mass.

The wall-clock cost of the verifier is negligible next to vLLM generation. End-to-end training time stays at about $20$ minutes, the same as OPSD.

\section{Analysis and Limitations}

\paragraph{Why HMMT moves and AIME does not.}
AIME24/25 were already the better numbers for local OPSD, so a sparse extra weight has less room there. HMMT25 is harder for this 1.7B checkpoint (base $23.1$), and local OPSD did not help it. Reweighting completed mistakes spends the same $100$ steps on traces that reached an answer and got it wrong, which is where a privileged teacher can actually correct the student. This is consistent with the scores and with the training statistics; we do not isolate a token-level mechanism.

\paragraph{Single-seed evaluation.}
The OPSD README notes that Avg@12 from one seed will move by a point or two. The $0.8$ AIME24 drop is in that range. The $5.0$ HMMT lift also appears in Pass@12 and majority vote. We only report one seed.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Results are for Qwen3-1.7B, LoRA, and $100$ steps. The verifier only sees a boxed span, so a wrong but well-reasoned trace with no box is treated like a truncation. The discrete $R$ is not token-level credit assignment. We did not add GRPO-style group advantages, a process reward model, or a different evaluation protocol. Scaling the same weight to 8B, to thinking-on student rollouts, or to a full GRPO token budget is left open.

\section{Conclusion}

OPSD already gives a dense, on-policy, single-model training signal, without using whether the student was right. Boxed-error reweighting multiplies that signal by a verifier weight and leaves truncated traces at weight one. Under a matched local budget, HMMT25 Avg@12 rises by five points; AIME24 and AIME25 do not. The change is a few dozen lines on the public trainer, and $\lambda{=}0$ restores the original algorithm.
